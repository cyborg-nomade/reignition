# Kek

<p class="article-meta">Tome IV · Abstract Horror / BLOCK 5 - HYPERSTITION · April 19, 2016</p>

[Read the original post ↗](http://www.xenosystems.net/kek/){ .article-source }

[![Kek00](https://i1.wp.com/www.xenosystems.net/wp-content/uploads/2016/04/Kek00-e1461082795377.jpg?resize=666%2C654)](https://i1.wp.com/www.xenosystems.net/wp-content/uploads/2016/04/Kek00-e1461082795377.jpg)

Of which it is
[said](https://desustorage.org/his/thread/312310/) (I do
not pretend to grasp more than a pitiful sliver of this): “Pepe has
guided humanity since time immemorial. This is Heqet, the frog-headed
Ancient Egyptian goddess, symbol of life and protector on the journey
to the afterlife. She guided the ancient Egyptians who transcended
normie-ism to a land of poorly drawn dick-girls and the dankest of
memes. A little known fact is that while normies evolved from the
famously social monkeys, those destined to browse dank memes alone in
dimly-lit rooms evolved from another species who also prefers dark
moist habitats, namely the frog.”

The name ‘Kek’
*[appears](https://knowyourmeme.com/memes/kek)* to
have crossed into Cyberspace by odd coincidence (and not — originally
— as a name at all). Orcish, Korean, and Turkish languages were all
supposedly involved. ‘Kek’ was an encryption of ‘LOL’ within certain
*World of Warcraft* communication channels. The Turkish ‘[Topkek](https://knowyourmeme.com/memes/topkek)‘ (a cup*cake* brand) was a secondary coincidence. No one
seems to have been invoking the chaos
[deities](https://www.thekeep.org/~kunoichi/kunoichi/themestream/kek.html#.VxanYkwrKUk)
of Ancient Khem at that point.

The introduction of Pepe — a manifest frog-entity avatar — is shrouded
in even greater
[obscurity](https://www.reddit.com/r/OutOfTheLoop/comments/31avpi/can_someone_explain_the_rare_pepe_phenom_to_me/). The memetic phenomenon was (again, *apparently*) convergent,
or coincidental — an entirely independent frog plague ([צְּפַרְדֵּעַ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plagues_of_Egypt), Exodus 7:25–8:15).

One more coincidence: Outbreak of the ‘[cuck](https://knowyourmeme.com/memes/cuck)‘ meme. (Kek is
[Kuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuk_(mythology)).) It’s
a definite ‘barbarous name of
[evocation](https://www.sacred-texts.com/oto/aba/chap9.htm)‘
in retrospect, but mostly still connected around the back. Kek, Kuk,
cake, cuck, might sound like consistent croaking, but tidy cultural
cladistics are difficult to identify. (A
[sense](https://www.radixjournal.com/journal/2014/11/18/a-god-to-damn-us)
of ethno-religious crisis on the Alt-Right is one indispensable
contextual element.)

[That](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuk_(mythology)) short
Wikipedia entry is worth citing in full:

***Kuk** (also spelled as **Kek** and
**Keku**) is the deification of the primordial concept
of darkness in
[ancient Egyptian religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Egyptian_religion). In the [Ogdoad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ogdoad)
[cosmogony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmogony), his
name meant darkness. As a concept, Kuk was viewed as
[androgynous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Androgyny),
his
[female form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammatical_gender)
being known as **Kauket** (also spelled as
**Keket**), which is simply the female form of the word
Kuk. […] Like all four dualistic concepts in the Ogdoad, Kuk’s male
form was depicted as a
[frog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frog), or as a
frog-[headed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head)
man, and the female form as a
[snake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snake), or a
snake-headed woman. As a symbol of darkness, Kuk also represented
obscurity and the unknown, and thus
[chaos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaos_(cosmogony)).
Also, Kuk was seen as that which occurred before light, thus was
known as the bringer-in of light. The other members of the Ogdoad
are
[Nu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nu_(mythology)) and
[Naunet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nu_(mythology)),
[Amun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amun) and
[Amaunet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amunet),
[Huh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heh_(god)) and
[Hauhet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heh_(god)).*

I’m heavily reliant on the commentariat here to sort all this out.
