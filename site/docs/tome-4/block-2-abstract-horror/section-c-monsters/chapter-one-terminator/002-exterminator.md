# Exterminator

<p class="article-meta">Tome IV · Abstract Horror / BLOCK 2 - ABSTRACT HORROR / SECTION C - MONSTERS / CHAPTER ONE - TERMINATOR · August 8, 2014</p>

[Read the original post ↗](http://www.xenosystems.net/exterminator/){ .article-source }

Gnon — known to some
[depraved](https://carcinisation.com/)
[cults](https://hurlock-151.tumblr.com/) as ‘The Great
Crab-God’ — is
[harsh](https://www.humanbiologicaldiversity.com/articles/Weiss,%20Volkmar.%20%22The%20Population%20Cycle%20Drives%20Human%20History%20_%20from%20a%20Eugenic%20Phase%20into%20a%20Dysgenic%20Phase%20and%20Eventual%20Collapse.%22%20The%20Journal%20of%20Social,%20Political%20and%20Economic%20Studies%2032%20(2007).pdf), and when formulated with rigorous skepticism, necessarily real.
Yet this pincering cancerous abomination is laughter and love, in
comparison to the shadow-buried
[horror](https://www.xenosystems.net/abstract-horror-note-1/)
which lurks behind it. We now understand that the silence of the
galaxies is a message of ultimate ominousness. A thing there is,
of incomprehensible power, that takes intelligent life for its
prey. ([This](https://waitbutwhy.com/2014/05/fermi-paradox.html)
popularization is very competently done.)

Robin Hanson, who tries to be cheerful, writes about it
[here](https://hanson.gmu.edu/greatfilter.html), and
talks about it
[here](https://www.facebook.com/pages/Urban-Future/448121928641155). Behind the smile (and the dopey interviewer), an abyss of dark
lucidity yawns. Some scruffy take-aways:

(1) UFAI panic is a
[distraction](https://meteuphoric.wordpress.com/2010/11/11/sia-says-ai-is-no-big%C2%A0threat/)
from this Thing. Unless the most preposterous
[paperclipper](https://www.xenosystems.net/against-orthogonality/)
scenarios are entertained, Singularity cannot matter to it (as
even paperclipper-central
[agrees](https://lesswrong.com/lw/g1s/ufai_cannot_be_the_great_filter/)). The silence of the galaxies is not biased to organic life —
there is no intelligent signal from anything. The first sentient
event for any true AI — friendly or unfriendly — would be the
soul-scouring cosmic horror of intellectual encounter with the
Great Filter. (If we want an alliance with
[Pythia](https://www.xenosystems.net/pythia-unbound/),
this would make a good topic of conversation.) The same
consideration applies to all techno-positive X-risks. Understood
from the perspective of Great Filter contemplation,
[this](https://www.wired.co.uk/news/archive/2014-08/07/10-qs-about-nasa-impossible-drive)
sort of thing is a trigger for raw terror.

(2) The Great Filter does not merely hunt and harm, it
exterminates. It is an absolute threat. The technical
civilizations which it aborts, or later slays, are not badly
wounded, but eradicated, or at least crippled so fundamentally
that they are never heard of again. Whatever this utter ruin is,
it happens *every single time*. The mute scream from the
stars says that nothing has *ever* escaped it. Its kill
performance is flawless. Tech-Civilization death sentence with
probability 1.

(3) The thread of hope, which would put the Exterminator behind
us, is highly science-sensitive. As our knowledge has increased,
it has steadily attenuated. This is an empirical matter (without
*a priori* necessity). Life *could* have been
complicated, chemically or thermically highly-demanding, even
resiliently mysterious. In fact it is comparatively simple,
cosmically cheap, physically predictable. Planets
*could* have been rare (they are super-abundant).
Intelligence *could* have presented peculiar evolutionary
challenges, but there are no signs that it does. The scientific
trend is to futurize the Exterminator. (This is very bad.)

(4) If the Great Filter finds mythological expression in the
hunter, it is only in a specific sense — although an
anthropologically realistic one. It is the hunter that drives to
[extinction](https://archaeology.about.com/od/mterms/g/megaextinct.htm). The Exterminator.

(5) We know *that* The Exterminator exists, but nothing at
all about *what* it is. This makes it the archetype of
horroristic ontology.
