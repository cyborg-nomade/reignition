# Quote note (#173)

<p class="article-meta">Tome IV · Abstract Horror / BLOCK 2 - ABSTRACT HORROR / SECTION A - HORRORISM / CHAPTER TWO - PRACTICE · July 15, 2015</p>

[Read the original post ↗](http://www.xenosystems.net/quote-note-173/){ .article-source }

Within the next half-century, the American West Coast faces a far
from insignificant threat of
[massive](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2015/07/20/the-really-big-one)
geological calamity:

*When the next very big earthquake hits, the northwest edge of
the continent, from California to Canada and the continental
shelf to the Cascades, will drop by as much as six feet and
rebound thirty to a hundred feet to the west — losing, within
minutes, all the elevation and compression it has gained over
centuries. Some of that shift will take place beneath the ocean,
displacing a colossal quantity of seawater. … The water will
surge upward into a huge hill, then promptly collapse. One side
will rush west, toward Japan. The other side will rush east, in
a seven-hundred-mile liquid wall that will reach the Northwest
coast, on average, fifteen minutes after the earthquake begins.
By the time the shaking has ceased and the tsunami has receded,
the region will be unrecognizable. Kenneth Murphy, who directs
FEMA’s Region X, the division responsible for Oregon,
Washington, Idaho, and Alaska, says, “Our operating assumption
is that everything west of Interstate 5 will be toast.”*

*In the Pacific Northwest, everything west of Interstate 5
covers some hundred and forty thousand square miles, including
Seattle, Tacoma, Portland, Eugene, Salem (the capital city of
Oregon), Olympia (the capital of Washington), and some seven
million people. When the next full-margin rupture happens, that
region will suffer the worst natural disaster in the history of
North America.*

Realistic accommodation to the prospect of black
[swan](https://www.zerohedge.com/news/2015-07-15/black-swan-taleb-warns-%E2%80%9Ccalm-storm%E2%80%9D)
events is psychologically — and even epistemologically —
impossible. It’s worth trying to hold onto the thought, however,
that unpredictable, singular
[events](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1755_Lisbon_earthquake), utterly senseless within the principal narrative structures of
human history, could at any point throw all expectations for the
ordered unfolding of developments off a cliff.
