# Oil Pulse (II)

<p class="article-meta">Tome III · Xenosystems / BLOCK 4 - CYBERNETICS / SECTION B - XENOECONOMICS / CHAPTER THREE - ENERGETIC RHYTHMS · January 30, 2015</p>

[Read the original post ↗](http://www.xenosystems.net/oil-pulse-ii/){ .article-source }

Given two finite natural commodities, one a consumable energy
resource undergoing accelerating absolute depletion, the other an
indestructible precious metal, there can be no question about the
fundamental trend of price divergence, surely? Except, apparently
there can. Pure reason (or principled intuition) fails once again:

[![Oil01](https://i2.wp.com/www.xenosystems.net/wp-content/uploads/2015/01/Oil01.jpg?resize=600%2C440)](https://i2.wp.com/www.xenosystems.net/wp-content/uploads/2015/01/Oil01.jpg)

The world seems determined to thrash us into empiricism.

([Via](https://www.zerohedge.com/news/2015-01-29/oil-hasnt-been-cheap-1988).)

If there is a trend, it shows up more persuasively in the erratic
[sequence](https://www.zerohedge.com/news/2015-01-29/biggest-hamilton-oil-shock-history)
of consistently-escalating negative oil price shocks.

[ADDED](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hotelling%27s_rule): Patri Friedman helpfully points to Hotelling’s Rule.
