# Greatness

<p class="article-meta">Tome III · Xenosystems / BLOCK 3 - TECHNO-COMMERCIALISM · March 23, 2015</p>

[Read the original post ↗](http://www.xenosystems.net/greatness/){ .article-source }

The problem with
[greatness](https://thediplomat.com/2015/03/singapore-and-the-worldview-of-lee-kuan-yew/)
is that nowhere near enough of it comes along to rely on. To assume
it, therefore, is a prospective vice, even if it is (retrospectively)
indispensable to historical understanding. It would be more convenient
for everybody if it could be ignored completely. This is one of those
moments in which it clearly cannot be.

[![LKY0](https://i1.wp.com/www.xenosystems.net/wp-content/uploads/2015/03/LKY0-e1427118095664.jpg?resize=666%2C375)](https://i1.wp.com/www.xenosystems.net/wp-content/uploads/2015/03/LKY0-e1427118095664.jpg)

The important things to note about Lee Kuan Yew have all been said
innumerable times before, and
[again](https://www.dannyquah.com/writings/en/2015/03/23/lee-kuan-yewan-appreciation-he-broke-the-model/)
in the last few days. He was a
[Neoreactionary](https://www.moreright.net/rip-lee-kuan-yew/)
before anybody knew what that was, an autocratic enabler of freedom,
an HBD-realist multiculturalist, a secessionist Anglospherean, and the
teacher of Deng Xiaoping. Right now, it’s tempting to be glib in
proclaiming him the greatest statesman of modern times — but he almost
certainly
[was](https://foreignpolicy.com/2015/03/22/long-live-lee-kuan-yew-s-lion-city-singapore/):

*In the 1950s and ’60s, Lee traveled from Sri Lanka to Jamaica
looking for success stories of former British colonies to emulate.
Fortunately, he chose different models instead: He decided to study
the Netherlands’ urban planning and land reclamation, and the oil
and gas giant Royal Dutch Shell’s management structure and
scenario-led strategy-making. Singapore, it is often joked, is the
world’s best-run company. Lee is the reason why. […] … Now the
yardstick is not personality but institutions. Lee Kuan Yew-ism, not
Lee Kuan Yew. This is why the 21st century belongs to him more than
to icons of Western democracy like Thomas Jefferson or even Jean
Monnet, the founding father of the European Union.*

[interesting](https://infoproc.blogspot.hk/2015/03/lee-kwan-yew-dead-at-91.html)

out there that are definitely worth a look, but mostly even the

[2](https://www.economist.com/news/asia/leekuanyew?fsrc=scn/tw/te/bl/ed/LKY),
