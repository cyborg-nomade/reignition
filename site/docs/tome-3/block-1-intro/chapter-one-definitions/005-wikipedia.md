[Universal Darwinism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_Darwinism)
([via](https://twitter.com/HbdNrx)): “**Universal Darwinism**
(also known as **generalized Darwinism**,
**universal selection theory**, or
**Darwinian metaphysics**) refers to a variety of
approaches that extend the theory of
[Darwinism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darwinism)
beyond its original domain of
[biological evolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evolution)
on Earth. Universal Darwinism aims to formulate a generalized
version of the mechanisms of
[variation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetic_diversity),
[selection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_selection)
and
[heredity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heredity)
proposed by
[Charles Darwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Darwin), so that they can apply to explain
[evolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evolution_(disambiguation))
in a wide variety of other domains, including
[psychology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychology),
[economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economics),
[culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture),
[medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medicine),
[computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science)
and [physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physics). …”

[Galton’s problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galton%27s_problem)
([via](https://twitter.com/KirkegaardEmil)): “**Galton’s problem**, named after Sir
[Francis Galton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Galton), is the problem of drawing inferences from
[cross-cultural](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cross-cultural_studies)
data, due to the statistical phenomenon now called
[autocorrelation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autocorrelation). The problem is now recognized as a general one that applies to
all nonexperimental studies and to
[experimental design](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Design_of_experiments)
as well. It is most simply described as the problem of external
dependencies in making statistical estimates when the elements
sampled are not
[statistically independent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_(probability_theory)). Asking two people in the same household whether they watch TV,
for example, does not give you statistically independent answers.
The sample size, n, for independent observations in this case is
one, not two. Once proper adjustments are made that deal with
external dependencies, then the axioms of probability theory
concerning statistical independence will apply. These axioms are
important for deriving measures of
[variance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variance), for
example, or tests of
[statistical significance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_significance). …”

It is one of the
[Earth](https://en.m.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earth)‘s
[largest known eruptions](https://en.m.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_largest_volcanic_eruptions). The **Toba catastrophe hypothesis** holds that this
event caused a global
[volcanic winter](https://en.m.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volcanic_winter)
of 6–10 years and possibly a 1,000-year-long cooling episode. …”
