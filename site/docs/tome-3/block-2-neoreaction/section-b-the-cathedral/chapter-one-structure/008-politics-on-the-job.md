# Politics on the Job

<p class="article-meta">Tome III · Xenosystems / BLOCK 2 - NEOREACTION / SECTION B - THE CATHEDRAL / CHAPTER ONE - STRUCTURE · November 20, 2014</p>

[Read the original post ↗](http://www.xenosystems.net/politics-on-the-job/){ .article-source }

A
[bunch](https://www.businessinsider.com/charts-show-the-political-bias-of-each-profession-2014-11)
of
[charts](https://abonica.files.wordpress.com/2010/03/occupational_rankings1.jpg)
breaking down occupations by ideology are flying across the
Internet at the moment. Perhaps Robin Hanson
[started](https://www.overcomingbias.com/2014/11/conservative-vs-liberal-jobs.html)
it? (Linked by Cowan
[here](https://marginalrevolution.com/marginalrevolution/2014/11/conservative-vs-liberal-jobs.html).) Hanson includes a link to
[this](https://www.nytimes.com/2010/01/18/arts/18liberal.html?_r=0)
NYT article, which focuses upon the Left-orientation of tertiary
education, but that’s a huge, perennial topic in itself.

Hanson has his own theory on the subject, based upon differences
in risk orientation, but my favorite analysis was provided by
commenter
[adrianratnapala](https://www.overcomingbias.com/2014/11/conservative-vs-liberal-jobs.html#comment-1701010799):

*Most of the data on those plots can be explained by a rule that
says “People who who tell other people what to think for a
living lean left. Nearly everyone else leans (nominally)
right.”*
