# Castillo on Nrx

<p class="article-meta">Tome III · Xenosystems / BLOCK 2 - NEOREACTION / CHAPTER SIX - OTHERS · July 29, 2014</p>

[Read the original post ↗](http://www.xenosystems.net/castillo-on-nrx/){ .article-source }

From the perspective of an intrigued (and thoughtfully critical)
libertarian, Andrea Castillo offers an initial
[appraisal](https://theumlaut.com/2014/07/29/a-gentle-introduction-to-neoreaction-for-libertarians/)
of Neoreaction. It’s definitely the most dispassionate yet, and in
various ways the most perceptive (which isn’t to forget how
admirable Adam Gurri’s more obviously polemical
[engagement](https://theumlaut.com/2014/02/10/mencius-moldbug-is-a-technocrat/)
was).

The greatest structural merit of the piece is the firm positioning
of Mencius Moldbug at the foundations of the phenomenon. Unlike most
of the critical NRx commentary so far, Castillo has clearly read
Moldbug with some care. This is basically enough in itself to ensure
that something real is being seen.

Steve Sailer, who served Castillo unwittingly as a gateway into the
darkness, receives disproportionate attention given his manifest
lack of affiliation with NRx. Of course, he’s hugely-respected
throughout the reactosphere due to his rare refusal to stop ‘[noticing](https://isteve.blogspot.hk/2014/01/patton-oswalt-political-correctness-is.html)‘ upon firm request. Beyond the fact he hasn’t let the Cathedral
put his eyes out, however, there’s nothing very much to
differentiate him from mainstream American conservatism. Still,
Sailer’s presence in the piece does much useful work. In particular,
it helps to mark out the boundary controversies defining
contemporary libertarianism (the immigration topic prominent among
them).

Since she’s already got herself into trouble, it can’t make much
more to add that
[@anjiecast](https://twitter.com/anjiecast) was already
one of my favorite people in the world (remember
[this](https://theumlaut.com/2014/02/11/the-wired-mans-burden/)
for instance?). A little bit more now.
