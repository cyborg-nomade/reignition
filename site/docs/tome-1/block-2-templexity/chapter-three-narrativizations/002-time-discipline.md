# Time Discipline

<p class="article-meta">Tome I · Urban Future / BLOCK 2 - TEMPLEXITY / CHAPTER THREE - NARRATIVIZATIONS · September 16, 2014</p>

[Read the original post ↗](http://www.uf-blog.net/time-discipline/){ .article-source }

If you run through the functional specifications of your time
machine, and it looks as if it’s going to
[print](https://www.giantflightlessbirds.com/2012/10/nine-problems-with-looper-that-arent-brain-melting-time-paradoxes/)
bullion, or proliferate
[doubles](https://www.xibalba.demon.co.uk/jbr/babel2/),
it’s been badly assembled.
*Time-travel is the dramatization of something else*, and
you’re still trapped in the simulation.

*Forbes*
[on](https://www.forbes.com/sites/brucedorminey/2013/08/28/should-time-travel-be-a-moral-imperative/)
Seth Lloyd:

*In Type 1 time travel — the type highlighted in the
[“Back to the Future”](https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/back_to_the_future/)
films — all possible pasts and futures in some sense exist
simultaneously, says Lloyd. So, that when you go back and change
the past in order to enter a different future, your “old” future
is in some sense still “there.”*

*“From a theoretical physics standpoint,” said Lloyd, “Type 1 is
certainly possible, but we still don’t have a very good theory of
how it would work.”

He notes that current physical theory favors Type 2 time travel
scenario in which the past can’t be changed no matter how hard one
tries.*

*“Our theory of time travel is Type 2,” said Lloyd, “[which means]
no matter how hard you try to mess with the past you can’t do
it.”*

HP Lovecraft
[fixed](https://www.uf-blog.net/templexity/) the
principle.
