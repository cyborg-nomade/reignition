# Introducing Urban Future

<p class="article-meta">Tome I · Urban Future / BLOCK 1 - URBAN FUTURE · March 29, 2011</p>

[Read the original post ↗](https://oldnicksite.wordpress.com/2011/03/29/introducing-urban-future/){ .article-source }

What can readers expect from this blog? Since it promises to be
oriented towards the future, it makes sense to begin with some
preliminary forecasting about itself.

Most basically and predictably, Urban Future has been programmed by
its name. Its principal topic is the intersection of cities with the
future. It aims to foster discussion about cities as engines of the
future, and about futurism as a dynamic influence on the shape,
character, and development of cities. More particularly, it scavenges
for clues, and floats speculations, about the Shanghai of tomorrow. It
anticipates a global urban future in which Shanghai features
prominently, and a coming Shanghai that expresses, both starkly and
subtly, the transformative forces of global futurism. This is to get
quite far ahead of ourselves, which is where we shall typically be.

For some readers, ‘futurism’ will invoke the early 20th century avant
garde cultural movement crystallized by Filippo Tommaso Marinetti’s
1909
[Futurist Manifesto](https://cscs.umich.edu/%7Ecrshalizi/T4PM/futurist-manifesto.html). Futurism, they might reasonably object, has been defined and even
closed by the passage of time. Like modernism, it now belongs to the
archive of concluded history. What exists today, and in the days to
come, can only be a neo-futurism (and a neo-modernism): no less
retrospective than prospective, as much a repetition as a speculation.
Such considerations, corrections, and recollections, with all their
attendant perplexities, are extremely welcome. The time to address
them will soon come.

Since Shanghai is cross-hatched with the time-fractured indices of
historico-futuristic ambiguity, from
[paleo-modernism](https://www.collectionscanada.gc.ca/obj/s4/f2/dsk2/tape16/PQDD_0001/MQ29850.pdf)
to
[neo-traditionalism](https://www.etsy.com/storque/handmade-life/noted-the-landscapes-of-yang-yongliang-11955/), the blog will have every opportunity to discuss such things. For
the moment, casual reference to the strangely-twinned architectural
icons of such time-tangles, the Park Hotel and the Jinmao Tower – each
a retro-futurist or cybergothic masterpiece – has to substitute as a
mnemonic and promissory note.

Also, in time, the obstacles to forecasting need to be thoroughly
addressed: such topics as historical catastrophism, the
[efficient-market hypothesis](https://www.alvinhan.com/Efficient-Market-Hypothesis.htm)
(EMH), Karl Popper’s
[critique of historicism](https://www.amazon.com/RC-Bundle-Historicism-Routledge-Classics/dp/0415278465),
[Knightian uncertainty](https://web.mit.edu/newsoffice/2010/explained-knightian-0602.html)
(or Rumsfeldian “[unknown unknowns](https://www.slate.com/id/2081042/)”) and the Black Swan theory of
[Nassim Nicholas Taleb](https://www.fooledbyrandomness.com/). In order to get up and running, all these complicating thoughts
have been temporarily bracketed, like cunning and ferocious beasts,
but they will not remain caged forever, or even very long.

Because there’s something irresistibly twisted about starting with the
future, the first flurry of posts will head straight into tomorrow,
with topics becoming increasingly city- and Shanghai-focused as things
progress. An initial series of interconnected posts will outline
futuristic thinking in broad terms, including preliminary sketches of
principal way-stations on the
[mainline techno-scientific tradition](https://pespmc1.vub.ac.be/cybsacad.html)
that supports it.

Ultimately, nothing relevant to the future of Shanghai is alien to
this blog’s purpose. It will draw upon Shanghai
[history](https://web.mit.edu/lipoff/www/hapr/spring02_wto/shanghai.pdf), geography, and
[culture](https://www.amazon.com/Shanghai-Style-Design-Between-Wars/dp/1592650783), traditional Chinese philosophies of time ([Yijing](https://www.zhouyi.com/)
and
[Daoism](https://www.chinapage.com/gnl.html)), theories of
[modernity](https://www.cato-unbound.org/archives/november-2009-how-the-world-got-modern/)
and
[urbanism](https://www.complexsys.org/TISandersEssay.pdf),
[evolutionary biology](https://blogs.discovermagazine.com/loom/),
[science fiction](https://www.complete-review.com/reviews/stephenn/diamond.htm), techno-scientific discussions of
[complex systems and emergence](https://www.santafe.edu/), the economics of
[spontaneous order](https://www.econlib.org/library/Essays/LtrLbrty/bryTSO.html),
[long waves](https://www.academicjournals.org/jeif/PDF/pdf2010/Apr/Smihula.pdf),
[technological trends](https://ir.kaist.ac.kr/papers/2008/20081025_2009_SAC_Camera-ready.pdf),
[robotics](https://www.frc.ri.cmu.edu/%7Ehpm/)
research and developments, models of
[accelerating change](https://www.kurzweilai.net/the-law-of-accelerating-returns), and anticipations of
[Technological Singularity](https://www-rohan.sdsu.edu/faculty/vinge/misc/singularity.html). Things should get continuously
[weirder](https://accelerating.org/downloads/SmartEvoDevoUniv2008.%20pdf).

Tomorrow, it begins.
