It was written is sequence, so the overall structure could have been
tightened (in retrospect). Without external disciples — or at least
its interiorized simulcrum — it would probably have been extended to
five parts, or more.

The first part already contains the most pronounced conclusion. The
emergence of blockchain-based monetary systems intersects with the
geopolitics of world currencies, and will inevitably modulate their
deep historical rhythms. The RMB is less likely to become the
central world reserve currency in the blockchain-epoch, principally
because this status is a poisoned chalice, subtracting effective
economic control even as it cements nominal dominance.

Despite superficial political reservations, and some characteristic
patience (even inscrutability), the China factor is almost certain
to advance the introduction of the decentralized public ledger
*commercium*, which will organize the next-stage future of
the global economy. None of these claims strike me as seriously
controversial.
