# Modern Legacy

<p class="article-meta">Tome I · Urban Future / BLOCK 4 - SINGLOSPHERE / CHAPTER FOUR - URBAN DEVELOPMENT · September 1, 2014</p>

[Read the original post ↗](http://www.uf-blog.net/modern-legacy/){ .article-source }

In 2012 the global distribution of Internet connectivity still
looked strikingly
[Atlantean](https://www.uf-blog.net/war-without-end/):

[![internet01](https://www.uf-blog.net/wp-content/uploads/2014/09/internet01.jpg)](https://www.uf-blog.net/wp-content/uploads/2014/09/internet01.jpg)

Two years later, Alissa Walker at *Gizmodo*
[asks](https://gizmodo.com/a-map-of-every-device-in-the-world-thats-connected-to-t-1628171291/+alissawalker)
(rhetorically):

*Where is the internet? This map might explain it better than any
statistics could ever hope to: The red hot spots show where the
most devices that can access the internet are located.*   
On the new map, too, the Pacific Century has yet to dazzle. It seems
that infrastructure — even the most advanced digital infrastructure
— incarnates a legacy, rather than virtuality (or potential). By
accentuating the Internet-of-Things, the new map has actually dulled
the digitization of emerging markets, drawing vision back on a
retro-futural path to the historical roots of the modern world
order. (The color scheme also tends to under-emphasize the spiky
urban-concentrations of the Asian Internet, relative to the more
diffuse Euro-American distribution.)
