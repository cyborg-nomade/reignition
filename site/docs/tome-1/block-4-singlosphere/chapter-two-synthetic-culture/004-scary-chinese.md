# Scary Chinese

<p class="article-meta">Tome I · Urban Future / BLOCK 4 - SINGLOSPHERE / CHAPTER TWO - SYNTHETIC CULTURE · September 30, 2013</p>

[Read the original post ↗](http://www.uf-blog.net/scary-chinese/){ .article-source }

Jeffrey Wasserstrom
[conducts](https://www.thechinastory.org/2013/09/a-century-of-american-dreams-and-nightmares-of-china/)
a tour of Western dreams and nightmares of China. Whilst the span of
the oscillation is remarkable, he finds the bipolar syndrome itself
to be notably stable across time. The upswing — Wasserstrom suggests
— is associated with hopes that ‘they’ are becoming more like ‘us’,
but on the downswing:

*… when the Western China Nightmare is dominant, the risk is that
observers and the general public lose sight of how varied the
Chinese populace is and instead grow accustomed to demonised
images of China … filled not with flesh-and-blood Chinese
individuals but a horde of soulless mannequins. […] Stories that
dehumanise China’s population tout court are also periodically
published, though only rarely do they do so as overtly as a 1999
Weekly Standard article which described the Chinese people as
prone to ‘Borg-like’ group-think conformity.*

When calmly dissected and investigated, durable stereotypes usually
have something significant to say, about both their subjects and
their objects. Western sinophobia is an especially rich hunting
ground for cultural explorers, and the importance of understanding
it is only going to grow. *Urban Future* will be bringing
sustained attention to this same topic in the months ahead.
