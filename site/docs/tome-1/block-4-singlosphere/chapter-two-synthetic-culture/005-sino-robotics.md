# Sino-Robotics

<p class="article-meta">Tome I · Urban Future / BLOCK 4 - SINGLOSPHERE / CHAPTER TWO - SYNTHETIC CULTURE · September 18, 2015</p>

[Read the original post ↗](http://www.uf-blog.net/sino-robotics/){ .article-source }

Somewhere deep in the task-queue here (at UF) is a post, or article,
exploring the resonances between phobic Occidental responses to
Orientals and robots (as promised, unreliably, in
[this](https://www.uf-blog.net/scary-chinese/) post). Some
[grist](https://nakedsecurity.sophos.com/2015/09/15/could-a-robot-write-this-article/)
for the mill:

*Last week, the giant Chinese internet and gaming company Tencent
published an article on its news portal about the rising price of
consumer goods in China – not exactly earth-shattering news,
except that the article was written by a robot called
[Dreamwriter](https://finance.qq.com/a/20150910/019573.htm). […] Dreamwriter wrote the 1000-word article, using algorithms
that search online sources and data, in just 60 seconds. The
article quoted economists and highlighted trends in a style
indistinguishable from a human financial reporter. […] According
to the
[South China Morning Post](https://www.scmp.com/tech/china-tech/article/1857196/end-road-journalists-robot-reporter-dreamwriter-chinas-tencent), Dreamwriter’s article was the first robot-written news article
in the Chinese language.*

*The Morning Post quoted a Chinese journalist who said China’s
state-run media doesn’t give reporters much creative license,
which makes them easily replaceable by robot writers: “You know,
many reporters working for government-run newspapers across the
country usually copy and paste the statements and news press. They
are not allowed to express doubt or really investigate reports
against the authorities. So robot reporters could easily replace a
lot of Chinese reporters like this nationwide.”*
