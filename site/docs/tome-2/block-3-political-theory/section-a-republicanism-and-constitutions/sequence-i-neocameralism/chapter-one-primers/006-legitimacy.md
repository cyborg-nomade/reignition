# Legitimacy

<p class="article-meta">Tome II · The Dark Enlightenment / BLOCK 3 - POLITICAL THEORY / SECTION A - REPUBLICANISM AND CONSTITUTIONS / SEQUENCE i - NEOCAMERALISM / CHAPTER ONE - PRIMERS · August 5, 2015</p>

[Read the original post ↗](http://www.uf-blog.net/legitimacy/){ .article-source }

As the conclusion to a quality
[piece](https://www.huffingtonpost.com/kishore-mahbubani/singapore-world-successful-society_b_7934988.html?utm_hp_ref=world)
of Singapore gloating, Kishore Mahbubani outlines the crucial
principle of regime legitimacy that liberal-autocratic East Asia
is honing for the world:

*Singapore has its fair share of detractors. Its political
system was widely viewed as being an “[enlightened dictatorship](https://books.google.com.hk/books?id=E5k6AwAAQBAJ&pg=PT112&lpg=PT112&dq=enlightened+dictatorship+singapore&source=bl&ots=HClZvZGAdc&sig=yTbkA-7ZUvAIcxGV5ah7eXxnxTE&hl=en&sa=X&redir_esc=y#v=onepage&q=enlightened%20dictatorship%20singapore&f=false),” even though free elections have been held every five
years. Its media is widely perceived to be controlled by the
government and Singapore is ranked number
[153 out of 180](https://index.rsf.org/#!/) by
Reporters Without Borders in 2015 on the Press Freedom Index.
Many human rights organizations criticize it. Freedom House
ranks Singapore as “[partially free](https://books.google.com.hk/books?id=aWBA7rcvhrQC&pg=PA256&lpg=PA256&dq=Freedom+House+Singapore+%E2%80%9Cpartially+free%E2%80%9D.&source=bl&ots=JJQfDiRD7T&sig=vx1qSXo9xyhzCLkEp7ANbxaHGD4&hl=en&sa=X&redir_esc=y#v=onepage&q=Freedom%20House%20Singapore%20%E2%80%9Cpartially%20free%E2%80%9D.&f=false).” […] Undoubtedly, some of these criticisms have some
validity. Yet, the Singapore population is
[one of the best educated populations](https://time.com/3856834/worlds-best-top-schools-survey-asia/)
and, hence, globally mobile.
**They could vote with their feet** if Singapore
were a stifling “un-free” society. Most choose to stay.
Equally importantly, some of the most talented people in the
world, including Americans and Europeans, are
[giving up their citizenship](https://world.time.com/2013/01/31/mister-taxman-why-some-americans-working-abroad-are-ditching-their-citizenships/)
to become Singapore citizens. Maybe they have noticed
something that the Western media has not noticed: Singapore is
one of the best places to be born in and to live in.*
[*UF* emphasis]

Jacobinism is typically too lost in its own evangelical
universalism to recognize its limits in political philosophy and
in space, if not yet quite so demonstrably in time.
